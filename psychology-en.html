Psychology of Education

Understanding psychological processes helps create an educational environment where people can learn, develop, and connect with others.

About this area

The psychology of education explores how people learn, process information, sustain motivation, and build relationships in educational settings. This area helps take account of the psychological needs and characteristics of everyone involved in education, recognising the person behind learning outcomes—with their experience, needs, abilities, and individual pace of development.

Who this area is for:
Teachers, lecturers, leaders of educational organisations, learning-support specialists, and teams interested in creating a positive educational environment.
What this area supports:
A deeper understanding of the psychological factors behind learning and interaction, greater awareness of participants’ needs, and ideas for developing a supportive educational environment.

Key Focus Areas

Motivation and Engagement

Understanding the factors that support interest in learning, autonomy, and active participation.

Psychology of Learning

Considering information processing, cognitive processes, individual differences, and the conditions that support learning.

Interaction and Communication

Psychological foundations of relationships between educators and learners, feedback, collaboration, and constructive dialogue.

Well-being in Educational Settings

The importance of psychological safety, respect, support, and a balanced approach to the workload of everyone involved in education.
